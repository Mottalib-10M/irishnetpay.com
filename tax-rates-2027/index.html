Tax rates in Ireland for 2027, next to 2026

Tax rates in Ireland for 2027 were set by Budget 2027 on 6 October 2026 and written into the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026. Income tax stays at 20 % and 40 %, but the amount taxed at 20 % rises by €2,500, to €46,500 for a single person. The personal, employee and earned income tax credits each go up by €125, to €2,125, and the 2 % USC band now runs to €30,300, so that a full-time job on the new minimum wage of €14.94 an hour stays out of the 3 % band. Against that, employee PRSI rises from 4.35 % to 4.5 % on 1 October 2027. For a single employee on €50,000, the net effect is €691 more take-home pay over the year. Income above the band is taxed at 40 %. The two-income band is not one pot: each spouse keeps a band of their own, the higher earner €55,500 and the lower earner up to €37,500, limited to their own income, so a couple only reaches €93,000 if the lower income is at least €37,500. The employee and earned income credits together still cannot exceed €2,125 for someone with both types of income. The USC change is aimed at the minimum wage: a 39-hour week at €14.94 comes to €30,298 a year, which would have crossed the old €28,700 ceiling. With the new one, a full-time minimum wage earner pays €426 of USC in 2027 and takes home €26,734 a year. The PRSI rise is the 2027 step of the schedule the Government set in 2024 to fund the Social Insurance Fund; it applies to every euro of pay above the €352 weekly threshold, so it takes back part of the Budget gain from October.

The gain is not linear. Up to about €30,000 it comes mostly from the credits and the USC band; from €46,500 upwards the wider 20 % band adds its full €500 (€2,500 moved from 40 % to 20 %), which is why the gain levels off for higher salaries, minus a PRSI rise that keeps growing with pay. A married couple on one income of €70,000 goes from €54,001 to €54,787, €786 more, because both the band and the doubled personal credit rise.

What changes on your payslip, and when

The new bands, credits and USC ceiling apply from the first pay date in January 2027. Revenue issues an updated Revenue Payroll Notification to your employer at the start of the year, and your tax credit certificate in myAccount shows the 2027 figures. If your first January payslip still shows €44,000 as your cut-off point, it is usually a timing issue that corrects itself with the next pay; a cumulative basis means any overpayment comes back automatically. The PRSI rise appears on the first pay date on or after 1 October 2027. The minimum wage of €14.94 applies to hours worked from 1 January 2027.

Weekly social welfare payments rise by €10 from January 2027, and Working Family Payment income limits by €30 a week. The social welfare pages still show the 2026 rates; they will move to 2027 when the Department of Social Protection publishes the new rate tables in full.

What is not settled yet

The tax figures on this page are those of the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 as published on 19 October 2026. The Bill can still be amended in the Dáil and Seanad before it is signed, usually in December; this page will be checked against the Act and against Revenue’s 2027 tables when they appear. The PRSI step of October 2027 is part of a schedule that the Government has said it will review after the 2027 actuarial review of the Social Insurance Fund.

Common questions

What are the tax bands in Ireland for 2027?

Income tax stays at 20 % and 40 %. The 20 % band rises by €2,500: €46,500 for a single person, €50,500 for a single parent with the Single Person Child Carer Credit, €55,500 for a married couple or civil partners with one income, and up to €93,000 for a couple with two incomes, the second band being capped at €37,500 or the lower income.

How much are tax credits in 2027?

The personal tax credit rises from €2,000 to €2,125 for a single person and from €4,000 to €4,250 for a married couple. The employee (PAYE) credit and the earned income credit rise to €2,125. The Home Carer credit goes to €2,050. The Single Person Child Carer Credit stays at €1,900.

How much more will I take home in 2027?

On €50,000 as a single PAYE employee, take-home pay goes from €39,648 to €40,339 over the year, €691 more, or about €58 a month. The gain comes from the wider 20 % band, the €250 of extra credits and the wider 2 % USC band, less the PRSI increase of 1 October 2027.

Does PRSI go up in 2027?

Yes, on 1 October 2027. Employee PRSI rises from 4.35 % to 4.5 %, the fourth step of the increase agreed for 2024 to 2028, and employer rates rise by the same 0.15 point. From 1 January 2027 the weekly pay at which the higher employer rate starts moves from €552 to €600, to follow the new minimum wage.

Is the Budget 2027 tax package law yet?

The income tax and USC changes are in the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026, published on 19 October 2026, which must be enacted before the end of the year to apply from 1 January 2027. The minimum wage of €14.94 is already set by statutory instrument. Revenue updates its tables and your tax credit certificate for January once the Act is signed.
